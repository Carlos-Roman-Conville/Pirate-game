# Story bible (kept by the story keeper; Rex is the author)

Rex writes the story, the characters and their development. This file **records** what he decides. Every item is
marked:

- **(Rex)**: his, canon.
- **(proposed)**: someone else's starting point; not canon until Rex says yes.
- **(open)**: nothing decided yet.

Seeded 2026-10-26 from the spec only (`../PIRATE.md`, `../crew/ROSTER.md`, `../STATS.md`); section numbers below
point there. Characters live in `characters/` (one file each, `characters/INDEX.md` lists them); the art keeper's
work lives in `../art/`, not here.

## Premise

- **(Rex)** A Stardew Valley-style life sim spun off from High Roller: same principles and art style, scope
  narrowed to one ship. You romance psychotic pirates; the crew are all hot psychos (§ header, §1).
- **(Rex)** Gambling is the main way to earn money; there is an RPG side for combat (§1, §6).
- **(Rex)** Like Animal Crossing, the crew grows over time and you build a relationship with each crew mate (§1).
- **(Rex)** You start as small as possible, like Luffy on his tiny boat: just you on a small boat at the starting
  port. Minigames and money-making buy bigger boats; bigger boats hold more crew and reach other ports (§4a).
- **(Rex)** The early game's central plot is **tending the ship**: morning duties, each its own minigame, or
  talking to crew mates instead (§5).
- **(open)** Working title "Pirate Ship"; the real name is Rex's (§12.1).
- **(open)** Who the player is: the captain (the spec assumes "captain's cabin (yours)") or a crew mate who rises
  (§12.5). Why they start alone on a small boat.

**Rex's goals for the cast** (what `JUDGING.md` judges against):

1. Hot psychos that a pirate setting makes permissible: the madness reads as a pirate doing the job too well
   (ROSTER intro).
2. Dangerous in different ways: each type's killer form, each madness its own (§7, ROSTER).
3. Dating doesn't fix them: getting closer to a possessive one can cause trouble with everyone else (§7a).
4. An interconnected crew: a relationship web, crew who act on each other without you (§7a).

## The world

### The starting port

- **(Rex)** Always the same port, the game's Pelican Town (§4a).
- **(Rex)** Run-down, full of dark characters, so low reputation is common there (§7b).
- **(Rex, 2026-10-26) The key theme that makes it believable:** the town is run down and in shambles, weakly kept
  together by the trade coming through, just barely. Houses are old and weathered from the sea; none of the
  buildings look new.
- **(Rex)** What keeps it together: a community that keeps living there, and the occasional ship that comes by to
  trade and spend its coin.
- **(Rex)** What keeps it afloat: it is a **small pirate haven**. It houses criminals, ne'er-do-wells and all
  types of schemers, the perfect place for lowlifes to thrive.
- **(Rex)** It is not a bustling port of pirates. Only low-life pirates come; a high-calibre pirate is extremely
  rare, because the quality of the women and the drink is absurdly weak.
- **(proposed, outside AI)** The economy as a loop: low-level pirates and fugitives come for shelter, anonymity,
  repairs and cheap entertainment; they spend what little they have; residents survive on it but can rarely
  improve anything; successful captains take their wealth elsewhere; the next desperate wave arrives. That is why
  it never grows into a real pirate port.
- **(proposed, outside AI)** The place: barely maintained docks; buildings repaired so often nothing matches;
  salt has eaten every exposed surface; one tavern, one shipwright in a failing workshop, a few merchants. The
  harbour holds fishing boats, damaged sloops and vessels that shouldn't still sail. A proper pirate ship arriving
  is an event the whole town notices, because someone with money has come.
- **(proposed, outside AI)** Most residents have a reason they haven't moved on (sample reasons: a first mate
  disgraced by a failed mutiny, a shipwright in debt to a local criminal, a gambler too indebted to leave, a
  surgeon who lost their licence or fled prosecution, an aspiring pirate nobody takes seriously, a raider too
  injured to sail, a smuggler with a good arrangement, a tavern worker supporting family). Some have no tragedy:
  they like the town, have family there, or are comfortable with its lawlessness, so not everyone is a disgraced
  outcast.
- **(Rex)** A real place, an ecosystem, not a hiring board: residents with no reason to leave (the blacksmith who
  has lived there his whole life; the bartender has little reason) plus visitors and people looking for work. The
  recruit pool is drawn from both (§7b).
- **(Rex)** About 12 recruitable, for the 3–4-berth boat: one of each core job, some doubles of common ones, and
  some cool-seeming jobs that don't fit the boat unless paired (§7b).
  - First mate: a choice between three.
  - Cannoneer (the sniper/mechanic slot): four options; one sniper-cannoneer is best for cannons alone.
  - Cook: a few overlapping options, plus one secret: someone who doesn't seem to know how to cook but is the best
    cook there, an ESTP who very clearly looks like your first mate. He is the old ESTP soldier (Rex, 2026-10-27;
    `characters/estp-m.md`).
  - Others: navigator, medic, sewer/clothcrafter, fisherman (numbers not set).
- **(Rex)** It has some recruits but not the full picture (§4a).
- **(Rex)** The town keeps a reputation with you, and the groups inside it do too; kidnapping someone a group liked
  locks you out of that group (§7b).
- **(Rex, 2026-10-27) Its places** (layout and upgrades kept by the town keeper in `../town/`): a bakery, a farm,
  a tavern/brothel, the docks (the shipyard, the repairmen and the market; the market starts at the port and leads
  into town), a small clinic, the apothecary, the church, perhaps a warehouse, the old coastal fort, and a locked house.
- **(Rex, 2026-10-27) Its people so far** (`characters/INDEX.md`): the ISFJ doctor at the clinic, the INTJ herbalist at the apothecary, the ENTP
  weaponsmith at the shipyard, the ISTJ head priest, the warehouse bigwig and his troubled child, the old ESTP
  soldier in the locked house, the INTP farmer, the ESFP baker.
- **(open)** Its name, its history, who runs it.

### Later ports

- **(Rex, 2026-10-26)** Ports have a hierarchy. The starting port is at the bottom, where low-life pirates go;
  the last level is a true pirate port, rich from the calibre of pirates coming through. (What sits between is
  open.)
- **(Rex)** A bigger boat lets you reach other ports nearby and trade (§4a). Sailing on a map, ports and time at
  sea come later (§5).
- **(proposed)** Each port one small screen: dock, tavern with tables, shop, notice board (§4).
- **(open)** Names, number, character of each.

### The ship tiers

- **(Rex)** You alone on a small boat → a slightly larger boat that houses 3 (a 4th can sleep on deck) → a ship of
  12–16 crew → 32–40 → and so on (§4a).
- **(Rex)** Each new crew mate's relationships layer on top of the ones you already have, so the web grows with the
  crew instead of being devised all at once (§4a).
- **(proposed)** Refits open new spaces on the larger ships: a bigger den, private cabins for romanced crew, a
  better brig (§4).
- **(open)** Names of the boats; whether the boats themselves have histories.

### Lore already noted

- **(proposed)** The den is contraband: many real pirate articles banned gambling for money on board (Bartholomew
  Roberts'), Appendix A.
- **(proposed)** Rumour and gossip carry facts about people ("Jimmie fixed up James's broken leg last year" is
  Rex's example of the form, §7b; the names in it are an example, not cast).

## Main arcs

- **(Rex, 2026-10-26) The central plotline: the starting port is dying.** It is already dying when you arrive.
  Fewer and fewer pirates come through; everyone is thinking of abandoning it; some stay despite it.
- **(Rex)** Your effect on it is either helping it thrive again or leading it to ruin.
- **(open)** Why it is dying (the rival port below is one proposed cause), and what thriving or ruin looks like at
  the end.
- **Endings and how the town changes:** kept by the town keeper in `../town/ENDINGS.md` and `../town/UPGRADES.md`
  (Digimon World 1-style upgrade slots whose combinations make the endings), not here.
- **(Rex, 2026-10-27) The questing model:** all four questlines are open to you; how you handle each path earns
  points, and crew who witness it shift alignment by their own inner rules. Details and each character's reactions:
  `QUESTLINES.md`.
- ~~**(Rex)** Who you take on each of the four questlines is how the game plays out.~~ Superseded 2026-10-27 by the
  questing model above.
- **(Rex)** Story by conditions: big storylines open when the ship's state allows them (a mutiny needs enough
  resentment, a replacement and a chance of winning), not as scripted branches (§7a, liked by Rex).
- **(Rex)** Things happen without you: crew start romances, alliances or mutiny plots; a romance may turn out to be
  manipulation (§7a).
- **(Rex)** Any crew mate can leave over their slight, with clues obvious, subtle or too late (§7b).
- **(Rex)** Three ways to recruit: hire, mercenaries, kidnapping (§7b).
- **(proposed, outside AI, 2026-10-26)** A central plot from the town's fragility: a richer pirate port starts
  drawing away the few ships that still visit; the tavern, the shipwright and the merchants lose trade, residents
  think of leaving, the criminals grow desperate. Residents split: attract wealthier pirates, go legitimate, raid
  ships themselves, or one ambitious character seizes the town to make it a real haven. The player's rise
  gradually crosses the town's fate.
- **(proposed, outside AI)** The town has its own trajectory; the player isn't expected to fix it. You might never
  come back, might worsen it by recruiting its most capable people, or might become its main source of trade.
- **(proposed, outside AI)** The return: a nobody in a town of nobodies at the start; later, rich and with a big
  crew, you come back to the same docks. The town hasn't changed; how its people see you has.
