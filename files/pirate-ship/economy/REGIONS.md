# Regions: the expansion roadmap, from the economy's side

What stays in which region, so that goods, drinks, crafts and skills do not blur across the map. Base game: the
Caribbean, 1650–1730. Rex's rule (2026-10-29): a region's goods and skills stay there; one reaches the Caribbean only
through a **rare-items trader**, **the odd ship**, or **a merchant's stall**, and a skill or recipe is never
manifested, only learned a real way (GOODS.md 3f). Marks as in GOODS.md: **(source)**, **(proposed)**, **open**.
Research notes: `research-regions-indian-barbary.md`, `research-regions-east-asia-malacca.md` (shared notes;
both partial, most prices open; the East Asia and Malacca notes lean on Wikipedia as pointers and on Dampier).

## 1. The tests (proposed)

A region earns an expansion of its own if it passes three tests, judged from the record and not from flavour:

1. **Its own goods and drinks:** things the Caribbean does not make and would want.
2. **Its own crafts and skills:** trades the Caribbean does not have, which a character could learn there.
3. **Its own ships and economy of piracy:** a different way of being a pirate, not the same game on a new map.

And one condition: **it overlaps 1650–1730**, so the base game's characters and knowledge rule carry over. A region
that fails the condition can still be an expansion, but as historical fantasy, apart from the timeline.

**Reading of the whole (proposed).** Four regions earn an expansion: Indian Ocean, Malacca, East China Sea, Barbary.
The order that follows the record is Indian Ocean first (the Caribbean's own pirates went there), then Malacca and
East Asia (which share the VOC and the junk trade and could even be reached in sequence), with Barbary apart because
its economy is a different thing. That is also Rex's note's preferred order, give or take the Mediterranean's place.

## 3. What stays in each region (source where marked; the lists are proposals)

The base game's goods, skills and drinks are GOODS.md. Nothing below is made in the Caribbean. Each region's crafts
would be new skills or foreign recipes under existing skills (GOODS.md 3f), learned only there, or from a rare
traveller.

### Expansion 1: Indian Ocean (Madagascar, the Red Sea, Surat, the Pirate Round)

- **Goods (source):** Mughal gold and silver coin and jewels (Every's Ganj-i-Sawai, "some 500,000 gold and silver
  pieces"; Tew's men "near three thousand Pounds a Piece"); diamonds ("sharing 42 small Diamonds a Man", England's
  crew); Surat silks (atlasses, taffetas, satins) and calicoes sold by the 27-inch cobit; painted chintz that
  "out-do all the Ingenuity of Europe" (Ovington); muslins; the Company's cloth in bales; Mocha coffee ("of very
  common growth in Arabia, especially about Mocha"); Gulf pearls; wootz steel exported as cakes; frankincense,
  myrrh, dragon's blood and aloe via Mocha; rice; cinnamon from Ceylon; Mozambique captives (200 taken by England's
  crew; the St Mary's traders sent slaves to New York). Open: cardamom, ambergris, cowries, Arabian horses.
- **Drinks (source):** arrack "distilled from Rice, and sometimes from Toddy"; toddy, "a Quart ... for a pice or two";
  coffee drunk the Arabian way.
- **Crafts, as skills or foreign recipes (proposed):** calico printing and painting (a Dye Making recipe set);
  wootz crucible steel (a Mining or Bladesmithing recipe); sewn, iron-free boats ("junks" at Johanna without nails, a
  Shipwrighting recipe); arrack from rice and toddy (the Distilling recipe already marked foreign in GOODS.md);
  pearl diving at the Gulf banks (Marine Gathering, known); the coffee house.
- **Ships (source):** dhows and grabs (Ovington), the Company's East Indiamen, the pilgrim ships; the pirates' own
  ships brought from the Atlantic.
- **Piracy (source):** the Pirate Round from New York and the Caribbean; St Mary's as the haven, Baldridge's post
  supplied by Philipse with naval stores, clothing and alcohol, paid in loot and slaves; ransom of the Viceroy of
  Goa at 2,000 dollars; shares in diamonds.
- **May leak to the Caribbean (proposed):** painted chintz and muslin (already row 34: pirates took them), Mocha
  coffee (the odd ship), a wootz blade (the rare trader), the arrack recipe (a far-travelled merchant). Everything
  else stays.

### Expansion 2: Malacca and the Spice Islands

- **Goods (source):** nutmeg and mace of Banda, "a VOC monopoly for almost two hundred years", the growers paid
  "1/122nd of the Dutch market price"; cloves of the Moluccas, which the Dutch could not wholly police; Sumatran
  pepper (Dampier: a Palembang junk "full laden with pepper"); Perak tin, in decline by 1700; Riau's tin, pepper and
  gambier; sago (Dampier at Mindanao: "common food ... is rice or sago"); betel; coconut oil (an Achin proa "laden with
  coconuts and coconut-oil"); ambergris of the Nicobars; trepang; birds' nests. Open: Bencoolen pepper, Sumatran gold,
  camphor, benzoin, rattan, sandalwood, cowries, opium, Indian cloth as currency.
- **Drinks (source):** Batavia arrack from red-rice yeast cakes and molasses, "distilled to about 70% ABV"; toddy
  (tuak). Betel chewing in place of drink at a chief's table.
- **Crafts, as skills or foreign recipes (proposed):** kris forging with pamor (a Bladesmithing recipe set);
  sago working (a Milling recipe); prahu building (Shipwrighting); batik and songket (Dye Making, Weaving; open in
  the research); Batavia arrack (Distilling).
- **Ships (source):** prahus, the Bugis padewakang of 20–50 tons that sailed for trepang; the VOC's ships.
- **Piracy and trade (source):** the VOC monopoly against smugglers; Bugis traders and mercenaries, strong at Riau
  after 1699; Johor; Dampier himself a gunner at Bencoolen.
- **May leak to the Caribbean (proposed):** nutmeg, mace and cloves (already imported-only in GOODS.md 1k, through
  Europe), a kris (the rare trader), Batavia arrack (the odd ship). The monopoly and the raw spice trade stay.

### Expansion 3: East China Sea (the Zheng network, Canton, Nagasaki)

- **Goods (source):** Chinese silk raw and woven; export porcelain of Jingdezhen (the VOC carried "30 to 35 million
  pieces" 1602–82) and Japanese Imari (a Dejima shop from 1662); tea (the first Company order 1667; in the 1660s "six
  to ten times more expensive than coffee"); tutenag; nankeen; Japanese silver and copper (what the Dutch bought silk
  with); camphor; Taiwan sugar (about 1,194,000 kg a year by 1683) and deerskins (151,400 to Japan in 1638); Japanese
  lacquer, paper and swords. Open: musk, rhubarb, fans, gold, tea quantities.
- **Drinks (source):** sam shu and hoc shu (Dampier); the Bashee islanders' sugar-cane drink (Dampier); tea. Sake
  and huangjiu: open in the research.
- **Crafts, as skills or foreign recipes (proposed):** porcelain making, a skill of its own (kaolin, the kiln:
  nothing in the Caribbean comes near it); silk reeling (Spinning recipe); tea processing (a Farming or Preserving
  recipe); lacquer (a Cabinetmaking recipe); junk building with watertight bulkheads (Dampier describes them;
  Shipwrighting); Chinese cannon casting (Founding; open).
- **Ships (source):** ocean junks with bulkheads and cane sails; war junks (open); Zheng Zhilong's "about 400
  junks" by 1627.
- **Piracy (source):** a different scale: Zheng sold protection passes and levied "water fees"; Tungning charged
  a passage tax and held the sugar and deerskin monopoly; the Qing sea ban and coastal evacuation 1661–83; the
  English treaty with Zheng Jing 1670; Canton opened 1684.
- **May leak to the Caribbean (proposed):** tea (already imported-only, 1k), a porcelain piece or a lacquer box
  (the rare trader or a merchant's stall), a Japanese blade (the rare trader). Silk reaches the Caribbean only as
  finished cloth (row 34). The crafts never leave.

### Expansion 4: the Barbary coast (Algiers, Tunis, Tripoli, Salé)

- **Goods (source):** captives and their ransom (Pitts "sold at the Bedesten"; a 1604 Mercedarian mission paid
  5,000 escudos in gold plus 6,000 in cloth and goods for 110 people, from a search page only); prize cargoes
  (over 1,000 ships taken 1608–34; Algiers' prosperity "depended on the corsairs' loot"); coral of the Algerian
  coast under a French monopoly; wheat; Levant returns through Smyrna and Aleppo: raw silk, cotton wool and yarn,
  currants, raisins, indigo, galls, camlets, maroquins; morocco leather, goatskin tanned with sumac, dyed red or
  black. Open: figs, dates, wax, ostrich feathers, tobacco, carpets, couscous, boza, ransom prices for the period.
- **Drinks:** no wine in the Islamic ports (the renegades' and captives' narratives); coffee houses; boza: open.
- **Crafts, as skills or foreign recipes (proposed):** morocco tanning and dyeing (Tanning and Dye Making recipes);
  galley and xebec building (Shipwrighting); coral working (open).
- **Ships (source):** the xebec, lateen-rigged, 200 tons or less; galleys and galliots; round ships brought by
  Dutch and English renegades.
- **Piracy (source):** state corsairing under the pasha or dey, who took a tenth of prizes (a weak source); the
  ransom economy with the redemptionist orders; the English at Tangier 1661–84 and the Algiers treaty of 1682.
- **May leak to the Caribbean (proposed):** morocco leather (a merchant's stall), coral (the rare trader), a
  captive's tale. The ransom economy is the region's own and is the thing to decide on (Open 1).

## 4. How the data keeps regions apart (proposed)

- Every good, recipe and skill carries `region` (`caribbean` by default); the four expansions are `indian-ocean`,
  `malacca`, `east-china-sea`, `barbary`. A recipe of one region is not in another region's recipe set.
- A region's good appears in the Caribbean only as an **import** with no recipe (GOODS.md 1k's "Imported only"
  list is the first of these: the Asian spices, tea, Peruvian bark, arrack), supplied by the three channels Rex
  named: the rare-items trader, the odd ship, the merchant's stall. The engine's supply for such a good is a trickle,
  never production.
- A region's craft arrives as **foreign knowledge** on a recipe (`knowledge: foreign`) or as a new skill with
  `learnedBy` set to the real way (a teacher from that region, a book, a blueprint). A character who has not learned
  it cannot make it, whatever their band.
- Skills carry over between regions; knowledge, resources and facilities do not (GOODS.md 3f).
